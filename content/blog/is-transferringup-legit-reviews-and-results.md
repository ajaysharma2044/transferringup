## How to vet a transfer consultant, including us

The standards for this profession are public, and worth ten minutes of your time. The Independent Educational Consultants Association, the largest professional body in the field, requires members to sign its Principles of Good Practice every year. Professional membership takes a master's degree, at least three years of admissions counseling experience, and visits to dozens of campuses before an application is even considered, per IECA's published membership standards. HECA and NACAC publish similar ethics statements. Whether or not a consultant belongs to any of those groups, the tests inside those documents work on anyone.

- Anyone who guarantees admission is lying to you. Committees decide, and no outside company controls a committee. IECA members pledge in writing never to promise placement, and that pledge exists because the promise is the oldest scam in the industry.
- Anyone claiming connections or pull inside an admissions office is describing either a fantasy or a fraud. The 2019 admissions scandal was that exact pitch, carried out.
- Pressure to pay today, or claims of two spots left, should end the conversation. Legitimate consultants run on application deadlines, which are public, and have no reason to invent fake urgency.
- If the only reviews exist on the consultant's own site, treat every claim as unverified, because it is.
- An honest consultant can tell you, on the spot, who their service is wrong for. Refusing to name anyone is its own answer.
