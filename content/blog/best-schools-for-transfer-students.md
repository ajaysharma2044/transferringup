Michigan admitted 2,385 transfer applicants for fall 2024, out of 6,832 who applied, and enrolled 1,580 of them. Those figures come from the university's own enrollment tables. Harvard's transfer admit rate the year before was 1.4 percent. Two elite brands, and one of them wants you while the other is politely holding the door shut. Picking the best schools for transfer students comes down to telling those two situations apart before you spend an essay on the wrong one.

I transferred to Cornell after a 2.9 high school GPA, and list construction is where I spend the most hours with clients, because it decides more outcomes than any essay edit ever will. What follows is the rundown I give across the table: the schools whose admissions operations are built to take transfers, with the newest rates and class sizes I can source, followed by the ones whose numbers say to walk away.

A few of these deserve commentary, because the row understates the opportunity.

UVA sits near 29 percent on its own, and then Virginia adds a contract on top: community college students who finish an associate degree in the VCCS system with a 3.4 hold guaranteed admission to the College of Arts and Sciences. Written down, signed, enforceable. If you are in Virginia at a two-year school, that agreement should be the spine of your whole plan.

Michigan is the volume story among public elites. Applications climbed from 4,513 in fall 2020 to 6,832 in fall 2024 while offers held steady between about 2,050 and 2,385, per the university's Office of Budget and Planning tables. The rate is drifting down, but a school extending two thousand plus offers a year has a reading operation staffed to say yes.

USC is the volume story among privates. For fall 2023 it drew 9,448 transfer applications, admitted 2,310, and enrolled 1,335 in the fall term alone, with an admitted GPA band of 3.73 to 4.00, per USC's published transfer profile. It also maintains articulation agreements with California community colleges, which is rare courtesy from a private at this level.

Georgia Tech runs transfers through formal pathway programs, and it works: 71 percent of the students in its 2025 transfer class arrived through one, with an average GPA of 3.78, per Tech's 2025 transfer class profile. The residency split matters, 30 percent for Georgians against 17 percent for everyone else, so out-of-state applicants should treat it as a harder target than the headline suggests.

The UC system is the biggest single lever in American transfer admissions. For fall 2025 it admitted 27,845 California community college transfers, per the UC Office of the President, and six campuses will sign a Transfer Admission Guarantee before you even apply. Berkeley and UCLA sit outside TAG but still admitted transfers at 24 and 23 percent for fall 2025. One concrete example of how wide this lane runs: Santa Monica College alone sent 1,859 applicants to UCLA for fall 2025, and 34 percent got in, with admitted GPAs between 3.74 and 4.00, per UCLA's transfer profile.

Cornell anchors the bottom of the board at 9.3 percent, and it still belongs on it, because 670 offers and 503 enrolled transfers make it the only Ivy taking a transfer class of any size. Its state contract colleges, CALS, ILR, and Human Ecology, absorbed 226, 126, and 76 of those fall 2024 enrollees. I wrote up the mechanics in [my Cornell transfer guide](/blog/how-to-transfer-to-cornell), and the wider two-year route in [my community college to Ivy League guide](/blog/community-college-to-ivy-league-transfer-guide).

## The doors that barely open

Now the other half of the ledger, because these names eat application slots that should have gone to the board above.

Harvard admitted 1.4 percent of transfer applicants for fall 2023. Stanford came in at 2.0, Princeton at 2.5, Dartmouth at 2.1, MIT near 2.5, and Northwestern at 1.3, all per each school's own Common Data Set filings. Yale reported 2.6 percent for fall 2025. In seats rather than percentages, several of these schools are choosing a couple dozen students from thousands of files, and some of those seats are earmarked for veterans or nontraditional applicants before the general pool is touched.

Columbia deserves its asterisk. The commonly quoted 4.4 percent for fall 2023 covers Columbia College and the engineering school, while the School of General Studies runs a separate review built for students with a nonstandard path behind them. If your education includes work years, military service, or an interruption, GS changes your Columbia math entirely.

My rule with clients: you get one, maybe two applications from this group, and only where you can name the specific program pulling you there. Anything more is postage spent on lottery tickets.

## How to read these numbers

Residency shapes almost every public school row. UNC's 36.5 percent leans toward North Carolinians the same way its freshman class does, UVA reads in-state applicants more warmly, and the UC pipeline exists for California community college students first. Out-of-state applicants are not shut out, but your personal odds sit below the headline rate at every public on the board, and above it if you hold the right zip code.

The school inside the school matters just as much. Cornell took 226 transfers into CALS and 15 into Engineering in fall 2024. UVA's guarantee covers Arts and Sciences and leaves out engineering, commerce, and nursing. Georgia Tech's pathway programs each carry their own course requirements. Before you put a name on your list, check the rate and the seat count for the specific college and major you would enter, because the university-wide number can be off by a factor of ten in either direction.

And the deeper pattern behind the whole table: schools with big transfer intakes budget for them. USC and NYU plan on students leaving and refill the seats every year. The UCs are legally structured around the community college pipeline. When a school admits transfers in the thousands, someone's job depends on filling those seats with people like you. When it admits twenty, nobody is looking for you at all.

## Building the list for spring and fall 2027

The shape I build with clients: two reaches taken from the lower half of the board, three or four schools where your GPA sits inside the published admitted band, and one structural anchor, meaning a TAG campus, the VCCS guarantee, or an articulation agreement you already satisfy. A 3.5 at a California community college reads Berkeley and UCLA as reaches and signs a TAG with, say, Davis or Irvine as the floor. A 3.8 at a four-year school out east reads USC, Michigan, and Notre Dame as targets and Cornell as the reach. Copying someone else's list is how strong applicants end up holding nothing but rejections in May.

Timing, since it is August 2026: spring 2027 applications close at many schools in October and November, and the main fall 2027 wave runs February through March 2027, with decisions from late spring. The semester starting now is the last full transcript the fall 2027 readers will see.
